Dispatch is in the details, and they change with the trailer

Load coordination, driver communication, broker relations and daily fleet management. What that means depends on what the truck is pulling.

Dry Van

  • Appointment or first come, confirmed before the truck rolls
  • In and out times on paper, so detention gets paid
  • Seal, lumper receipt and POD collected the same day
  • Reload planned before the trailer is empty

Reefer

  • Set point on the BOL matches the rate con, or we don't load
  • Pre-cool, washout and reefer fuel checked before pickup
  • Continuous or cycle, as the shipper requires
  • Pulp temperature noted at the dock

Power Only

  • Trailer number, location and interchange confirmed before hook
  • Coverage for a non-owned trailer checked first
  • Walk-around photos at pickup and at drop
  • Empty return or next trailer agreed in advance

How I read a rate con

A bad load usually shows itself on paper first. Open any line to see what I check before a truck is committed.

Sample document. Every name and number on it is fictional.

Rate ConfirmationSample
BrokerExample Freight LLC

MC number, phone and email domain against the FMCSA record. If the contact doesn't match, I call the number on file, not the one in the email. That is how double brokering gets caught.

PickupJoliet, IL. Tue 2:00 to 4:00 PM CT

Pickup number, live load or drop, and how long this shipper really takes. I plan on leaving at 4:00, not at 2:00.

DeliveryColumbus, OH. Wed 6:00 AM ET

The problem line. About 360 miles is roughly 7 hours of driving. If the driver came on duty at 7:00 AM CT, the 14-hour window closes at 9:00 PM, only 5 hours after loading. With a 10-hour break, the truck gets there about 10:00 AM ET. I ask for a later appointment before we book, not after we are late.

Weight43,500 lb

A reefer trailer is heavier than a dry van. I check it against the truck's empty weight and fuel, and have the driver scale close to the shipper.

Temp34°F continuous

It has to match the BOL. If the two disagree, I get the broker's answer in writing before the doors close.

DetentionAfter 2 hours, with notice

"With notice" decides whether it gets paid. I send it before the free time ends and keep the in and out times.

TONUNot stated

Missing terms are negotiated while the broker still needs the truck. I get it added to the rate con.

2:10 AM CT

Three calls, and what happens next

"Reefer is alarming and the temp is climbing."

  1. Alarm code, set point, return air and fuel from the driver
  2. Safe stop, restart by the book, read it again in 15 minutes
  3. Nearest reefer service on the line while we wait
  4. Broker informed with times and readings, all in writing

"I'm broken down and I deliver in four hours."

  1. Driver safe, exact location, photos of the problem
  2. Roadside called, and an honest repair time
  3. Receiver and broker told early, new appointment requested
  4. A second truck checked for a swap before it is needed

"They're refusing the load."

  1. Reason, name and photos before the driver leaves the dock
  2. Nothing moves until the broker gives instructions in writing
  3. Refusal written on the BOL, with times and the seal number
  4. Driver hours checked for the next stop or a safe place to park
